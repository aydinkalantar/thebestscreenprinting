## Prints that fill the shirt

A large format print is any graphic that takes over more of the garment than a typical chest, pocket or back hit. People call it jumbo, oversize or just a big print. Whatever the name, the effect is the same: the art becomes the garment.

It is the move for:

- **Streetwear and clothing brands** dropping a bold front or back graphic
- **Artists and tour merch** where the artwork is the whole point
- **Events, teams and clubs** that want lettering you can read from across the room

It is the same [screen printing](/screen-printing/) we run every day, scaled up with a larger screen and print area. That means solid ink coverage, consistent color from the first shirt to the last, and a finish that feels like screen printing because it is.

## Which garments take an oversize print best?

Flat, even surfaces give the cleanest big prints, so t-shirts and crewneck sweatshirts lead the way. Hoodies shine on the back; on the front, the pocket and seams set the limits. Tell us the garment and we will tell you what fits before you approve anything.

## How should I prep artwork for a jumbo print?

A big print shows every pixel, so file quality matters more here than on a small logo.

- Send vector files (AI, EPS, PDF or SVG) when you have them.
- For raster art, send a high-resolution file at the size you want printed.
- Call out Pantone colors if you need an exact match.

Every order gets a free art check. If something will break down at large size, we flag it before a single screen gets burned.

## Full-color art? You still have options

Screen printing is built for bold designs with a set number of ink colors. For photo-real or full-color art, look at [direct-to-garment (DTG)](/services/direct-to-garment/) or [direct-to-film (DTF)](/services/direct-to-film/). Both start at 12 pieces. Ask which one suits your print size and fabric.
