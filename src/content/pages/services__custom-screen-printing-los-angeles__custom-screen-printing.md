## Five steps from idea to ink

Screen printing rewards a clean start. Get the details right up front and the press does the rest. Here is exactly how an order moves through our Downtown LA shop.

### 1. Send the brief

Tell us four things: the design, the garment, the quantity and where the print goes (front, back, sleeve). Add your deadline. Use the [quick quote form](/quick-quote/) or email sales@tbsp.la. We reply within one business day.

### 2. Pick the blank

Choose from the [catalog](/catalog/) or bring your own garments (see [printing on your shirts](/services/custom-screen-printing-los-angeles/screen-printing-on-your-shirts/)). Tees, hoodies, long sleeves, tanks, totes and more all run on press. Not sure which tee? Our [favorite blanks](/the-best-custom-t-shirt-shop-in-la/) are a good shortcut.

### 3. Free art check and proof

Every file gets a free art check before anything is printed. We look for low resolution, lines too thin to hold ink, and colors that will shift on your garment. Then you get a proof showing placement, size and ink colors. Nothing goes to press until you approve it.

### 4. Screens and press

We separate your design into colors and burn one screen per color. Each color is printed in sequence, then the ink is heat-cured so it bonds to the fabric. Want a softer hand or a raised texture? See our [inks and effects](/services/custom-screen-printing-los-angeles/).

### 5. Pickup or delivery

Grab your order at 2710 S Broadway or have it shipped. Need it bagged, tagged or sent to several addresses? That is [fulfillment](/fulfillment/).

## What makes a design screen-print friendly?

- **Fewer colors, bigger impact.** Each color is its own screen, so a tight palette keeps the job efficient.
- **Solid shapes.** Bold type and graphics print crisp and opaque.
- **Real resolution.** Vector art or a large raster file at print size.
- **Gradients are possible.** Halftones can fake a fade. Read more in [silk screen print styles](/services/custom-screen-printing-los-angeles/silk-screen-printing/).

## Is screen printing the right method?

If you need 24 or more pieces of one design, yes, almost always. It gives you the best price per piece and the toughest print. Smaller run or full-color photo art? Look at [DTG](/services/direct-to-garment/) or [DTF](/services/direct-to-film/). Stitched logo on polos or caps? Go with [embroidery](/services/embroidery/).
