## Ink is half the design

The screen sets the shape. The ink sets the feel. Pick the wrong one and a great graphic looks flat. Pick the right one and the same art feels like retail. This page is your field guide to the inks and effects we run on press in Downtown LA. For the basics of the method itself, start at [screen printing](/screen-printing/).

## The two workhorse inks

**Plastisol** is the industry standard and what our standard pricing includes. It sits on top of the fabric, covers dark garments with confidence and holds color matches tightly. Durable, bright, works on almost anything.

**Water-based** ink soaks into the fibers instead of sitting on them. The result is a print you can barely feel, which is why fashion labels and eco-minded brands ask for it. It shines on light cotton and organic fabrics with clean, low-detail art.

## Push your design further

Special effects turn a print into something people want to touch.

- **Natural discharge.** A discharge agent pulls the dye out of 100% cotton, leaving a soft, ink-free graphic on dark garments. Expect some variation from shirt to shirt; that is part of the look.
- **High density.** Built up with a special screen setup into a squared-off print that stands up to 1/8 inch off the fabric. Best on bold text and symbols, not fine detail or halftones.
- **Super gel.** Like high density with softer, rounded edges and a wet, glossy look. Usually clear, can be tinted. Great for drips and accents.
- **Foil.** A printed adhesive plus heat-pressed foil for a mirror finish. One foil color per print location, bold shapes only. Wash inside-out in cold water and hang dry.
- **Metallic.** A subtle sheen without the full shine of foil.
- **Puff.** An additive makes plastisol rise like dough. Soft, nostalgic, and a budget-friendly alternative to high density.
- **Glitter.** Metallic flakes in a clear base, on the whole design or one area.
- **Glow-in-the-dark.** A translucent overprint, best over white or light inks.

## How do you choose the right effect?

Start with the art. Bold shapes and type can take any effect. Fine lines, gradients and photo detail cannot take high density, gel or foil, so save those effects for logos and headlines. Then think about the garment: discharge needs 100% cotton, and glow needs a light ink underneath. Remember that high density, foil and discharge do not mix on one garment.

Want soft but punchy? Water-based or discharge. Want it to catch light on stage? Foil or metallic. Want texture people reach for? Puff or high density.

## What about color matching?

Puff, high density and glitter can be matched to Pantone solid colors. No color guide? We match your file to the closest available swatch. Every order gets a free art check, so we will flag any effect that will not hold up on your design.
