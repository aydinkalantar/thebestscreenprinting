There are several types of thread that can be used for embroidery, and the choice often depends on the specific project and the desired effect. Here are some common types of embroidery thread:

- Stranded Cotton: Stranded cotton is a versatile and commonly used embroidery thread. It is made of six loosely twisted strands that can be separated according to the desired thickness. It comes in a wide range of colors and is easy to find in most craft stores.
- Perle Cotton: Perle cotton is a non-divisible thread that has a slightly thicker and more lustrous appearance than stranded cotton. It is available in different thicknesses, usually labeled with a number, and is often used for decorative stitches.
- Rayon: Rayon thread has a shiny appearance and is available in a wide range of colors. It is often used for machine embroidery and for adding a touch of shine to hand embroidery.
- Silk: Silk thread is a luxurious and expensive option that is often used for high-end embroidery projects. It has a soft, smooth texture and a natural sheen.
- Metallic: Metallic thread is used for adding a metallic sheen to embroidery projects. It can be made of real metal or synthetic materials and is available in a range of colors and thicknesses.

Overall, the type of embroidery thread used depends on the project’s requirements and personal preference.
